# Explore your context

**Goal:** read a project's context the way you would read a dashboard. You
follow the KapiMart sample's rules and content across the axes the project
declares, move a window through time, find the rules that hold only for a
while, and compare two moments.

You need [Kapi Desktop](/kapi/get-started/installation#install-kapi-desktop)
and the KapiMart sample. No model runs and nothing you do here changes the
project. For what each section of the app is, see the
[overview](/kapi/desktop/overview).

## What the sample declares

The sample's `kapi.yaml` names four axes beside the product and the channel,
and says what kind each one is. The market is a geo axis: its values are
places, and the recipe declares a few of its own beside the countries and
counties kapi knows:

```yaml
places:
  nordics:
    name: Nordics
    of: [no, se, dk, fi, is]
  oslo:
    name: Oslo
    type: locality
    within: no-03
    centroid: [59.91, 10.75]
  grunerlokka:
    name: Grünerløkka
    type: neighbourhood
    within: oslo
  greater-oslo:
    name: Greater Oslo
    type: metro
    of: [no-03, no-32]

axes:
  market:
    kind: geo
    label: Market
  audience:
    label: Audience
    values: [shoppers, developers, partners]
  age:
    kind: range
    label: Reader age
    unit: years
  lifecycle:
    kind: ordered
    label: Lifecycle
    values: [preview, current, sunset]
```

The kind decides how a rule at one value reaches content at another. A rule for
the Nordic market holds in Norway and in Oslo, and a rule for readers aged 13
to 17 holds in content written for readers aged 14 to 16. The [project
file reference](/reference/project-file#typed-axes) lists the kinds, and
[Place content in markets and areas](/kapi/places) covers places.

An Oslo youth campaign sits at `market: oslo` and `age: 13-17`, with its
pick-up page at Grünerløkka. Besides the two assistant sessions you review in
[Review what your assistant
noticed](/kapi/desktop/recipes/review-what-your-assistant-noticed), the sample
arrives with six notes a teammate recorded and kept. One holds in Japan, one
for readers aged 13 to 17, one for the Oslo campaign's readers, and three hold
for a while: one is in force now, one has ended and one starts next month.

## Steps

1. **Open the sample and its Explore section.** On the home, click **Try the
   sample**, then open KapiMart and choose **Explore** in the Context hub. The
   page opens on the last 30 days, with the snapshot at today.
2. **Read the timeline.** Each bar counts what was recorded that day: terms
   entering the terms store, notes, and decisions. Drag across the timeline to
   choose another window, or pick **7d**, **90d** or **All**. The counts under
   the timeline follow the window: records in scope, units of content, what
   needs a person, and what changed state.
3. **Trace a value through the flow.** **Flow** opens with four columns:
   family, product, channel and state at the snapshot. Hover **Rule** in the
   first column. The same records light up in every column, so you see which
   channels the rules were seen in and where each stands. Nothing changes until
   you click.
4. **Keep a selection.** Click **Contested**. It becomes a chip under
   **Scope**, and every view now reads only those records. Shift-click
   **Suggested** to add it; values of one axis combine with *or*, and values
   of different axes with *and*. **Clear filters** empties the scope.
5. **Change the columns.** Use a column's menu to show another axis, such as
   **Market** or **Reader age**, and **Column** to add one. Arrows move a column
   left or right.
6. **Find the rules that hold for a while.** Under **Try a question**, choose
   **Rules with a lifetime**. The first column becomes **Lifetime**: one note
   **In force**, one **Expired** and one **Upcoming**. Select one to open it
   beside the view. The pane shows the scope it holds at, when it was recorded,
   the window it holds for, its history and the sentence it was seen in.
7. **Switch the clock.** Set **Clock** to **Effective**. The timeline now
   places a rule's window at the dates it opened and closed rather than the day
   it was recorded, so you see when the context changed rather than when
   somebody wrote it down. A window that opens after today is counted beside
   the legend as outside the span.
8. **See where the markets are.** Open **Map**. It draws the world by
   country: Japan and Germany are filled by the notes placed there, and the
   five Nordic countries are hatched, because the winter campaign note holds
   in the Nordics without sitting in any one of them. The list beside the map
   counts each place's records **Here** and those **Held** through a wider
   place, and gives the records at **World** as one **Held everywhere**
   count. Hover **Nordics** to outline its countries. Click **Norway** to
   drill into its counties, and hover **Greater Oslo** to outline the two
   counties it is made of. Click the county of **Oslo** to reach the places
   the recipe declares: the city is a circle at its centroid. The path above
   the map takes you back up. To keep only what holds in one place, select it in the list.
   **Grid** draws one equal tile per country instead, so tile area is not
   land area.
9. **Read state over time.** Open **Over time**. Each row is one value of an
   axis and each column a stretch of the window, coloured by the share
   waiting for a person at the end of that stretch.
10. **Compare two moments.** Click **Pin A**. Move the window back a month, then
    choose **Compare with A**. Both sides keep the same order of values and the
    same scale. The counts say how many records only one side holds, and how
    many of those both hold changed state.
11. **Take the investigation with you.** The download button saves the query,
    the pinned side, the selected record and the counts as JSON, so a teammate
    or an assistant can read what you were looking at.

## The same on the command line

```bash
kapi context atlas                          # a summary: axes, records, span
kapi context atlas --json                   # the data Explore reads
kapi context log <id>                       # one rule and every decision about it
kapi context note "Prices include tax." \
  --seen-in src/en/store-ui.json --quote "Estimated Tax" \
  --point market=jp --valid-to 2027-01-01   # holds in one market, for a while
```

A note's `--point` can only narrow where its evidence was seen: a note seen in
the store's strings can be placed in Japan, and placing it more widely is a
person's decision in review. `--valid-from` and `--valid-to` take a date or an
instant, and the window includes its start and excludes its end.
