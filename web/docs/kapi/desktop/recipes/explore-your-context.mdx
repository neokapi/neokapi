# Explore your context

**Goal:** read a project's context the way you would read a dashboard. You
follow the KapiMart sample's rules and content across the axes the project
declares, move a window through time, find the rules that hold only for a
while, and compare two moments.

You need [Kapi Desktop](/kapi/get-started/installation#install-kapi-desktop)
and the KapiMart sample. Nothing in this recipe runs a model or changes the
project. For what each section of the app is, see the
[overview](/kapi/desktop/overview).

## What the sample declares

The sample's `kapi.yaml` declares four axes beside the product and the
channel. The market is a geo axis. Its values are places: the built-in
countries and counties, and the places the recipe declares:

```yaml
places:
  nordics:
    name: Nordics
    of: [no, se, dk, fi, is]
  oslo:
    name: Oslo
    type: locality
    within: no-03
    centroid: [59.91, 10.75]
  grunerlokka:
    name: Grünerløkka
    type: neighbourhood
    within: oslo
  greater-oslo:
    name: Greater Oslo
    type: metro
    of: [no-03, no-32]

axes:
  market:
    kind: geo
    label: Market
  audience:
    label: Audience
    values: [shoppers, developers, partners]
  age:
    kind: range
    label: Reader age
    unit: years
  lifecycle:
    kind: ordered
    label: Lifecycle
    values: [preview, current, sunset]
```

The axis kind determines whether a rule at one value applies to content at
another. A rule for the Nordic market holds in Norway and in Oslo. A rule for
readers aged 13 to 17 holds in content written for readers aged 14 to 16. The [project
file reference](/reference/project-file#typed-axes) lists the kinds, and
[Place content in markets and areas](/kapi/places) covers places.

An Oslo youth campaign sits at `market: oslo` and `age: 13-17`, with its
pick-up page at Grünerløkka. The sample includes the two assistant sessions
you review in [Review what your assistant
noticed](/kapi/desktop/recipes/review-what-your-assistant-noticed). It also
includes six notes that a teammate recorded and kept. One holds in Japan, one
for readers aged 13 to 17 and one for the Oslo campaign's readers. Three hold
for a limited time: one is in force today, one has ended and one starts in 20
days.

## Steps

1. **Open the sample and its Explore section.** On the home, click **Try the
   sample**, then open KapiMart and choose **Explore** in the Context hub. The
   page opens on the last 30 days, with the snapshot at today.
2. **Read the timeline.** Each bar counts the records of one day: terms
   added to the terms store, notes and decisions. Drag across the timeline to
   choose another window, or pick **7d**, **90d** or **All**. The counts under
   the timeline cover the window: records in scope, units of content, records
   that need a person, and records that changed state.
3. **Trace a value through the flow.** **Flow** opens with four columns:
   family, product, channel and state at the snapshot. Hover **Rule** in the
   first column. The same records are highlighted in every column, which
   shows the channels where the rules were seen and the state of each. The
   scope stays as it is until you click.
4. **Keep a selection.** Click **Contested**. It becomes a chip under
   **Scope**, and every view shows only those records. Shift-click
   **Suggested** to add it. Values of one axis combine with *or*, and values
   of different axes with *and*. **Clear filters** empties the scope.
5. **Change the columns.** Use a column's menu to show another axis, such as
   **Market** or **Reader age**. Use **Column** to add a column, and the
   arrows to move a column left or right.
6. **Find the rules that hold for a while.** Under **Try a question**, choose
   **Rules with a lifetime**. The first column becomes **Lifetime**: one note
   **In force**, one **Expired** and one **Upcoming**. Select one to open it
   beside the view. The pane shows the scope it holds at, when it was recorded,
   the window it holds for, its history and the sentence it was seen in.
7. **Switch the clock.** Set **Clock** to **Effective**. With this clock,
   the timeline places a rule at the dates its window opens and closes, and
   ignores the day it was recorded. The timeline then shows when the context
   changed. A window that opens after today is counted beside the legend as
   outside the span.
8. **See where the markets are.** Open **Map**. It draws the world by
   country. Japan and Germany are filled, because notes are placed there. The
   five Nordic countries are hatched, because the winter campaign note is
   placed in the Nordics and holds in each of them. For each place, the list
   beside the map counts the records placed there (**Here**) and the records
   held there through a wider place (**Held**). The records at **World** form
   one **Held everywhere** count. Hover **Nordics** to outline its countries.
   Click **Norway** to drill into its counties, and hover **Greater Oslo** to
   outline the two counties it is made of. Click the county of **Oslo** to
   show the places the recipe declares in it. The city is drawn as a circle
   at its centroid. Use the path above the map to go back up. To keep only
   the records that hold in one place, select the place in the list. **Grid**
   draws one tile of equal size per country, so tile area is unrelated to
   land area.
9. **Read state over time.** Open **Over time**. Each row is one value of an
   axis and each column is a stretch of the window. A cell's colour is the
   share of records that need a person at the end of that stretch.
10. **Compare two moments.** Click **Pin A**. Move the window back a month, then
    choose **Compare with A**. Both sides keep the same order of values and the
    same scale. The counts give the number of records on only one side, and
    the number of records on both sides whose state changed.
11. **Take the investigation with you.** The download button saves the query,
    the pinned side, the selected record and the counts as JSON. A teammate
    or an assistant can read the file to see what you were looking at.

## The same on the command line

```bash
kapi context atlas                          # a summary: axes, records, span
kapi context atlas --json                   # the data Explore reads
kapi context log <id>                       # one rule and every decision about it
kapi context note "Prices include tax." \
  --seen-in src/en/store-ui.json --quote "Estimated Tax" \
  --point market=jp --valid-to 2027-01-01   # holds in one market, for a while
```

A note's `--point` can only narrow the place where its evidence was seen. A
note seen in the store's strings can be placed in Japan. Placing it more
widely is a person's decision in review. `--valid-from` and `--valid-to` take
a date or an instant. The window includes its start and excludes its end.
