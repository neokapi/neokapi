# Review what your assistant noticed

**Goal:** practise the loop you run after every assistant session, on a
project where nothing you do matters. You decide about what two assistants
noticed in the KapiMart sample, see a kept rule reach the assistant, and go
back to before a session.

The sample arrives with the notes already recorded, so no model runs and you
need no assistant of your own. You need [Kapi
Desktop](/kapi/get-started/installation#install-kapi-desktop) and nothing
else. For what each surface is, see the [overview](/kapi/desktop/overview).

## Steps

1. **Open the sample.** On the home, under **Start here**, click **Try the
   sample**. kapi creates KapiMart, reads its voice profile, terms and content
   memory into your workspace, and records two assistant sessions against its
   files: claude-code first, then codex.
2. **Read the news on the home.** **What's new** has a row for KapiMart. It
   starts with the mark of the assistant that worked there last, CX for codex,
   and carries a chip for each kind of thing it noticed, such as "1 name" and
   "1 word". Because the two assistants disagree about one wording, the row
   adds a highlighted "1 needs you" chip and its button reads **Decide**.
3. **Open What's new.** Click **Decide**. The project opens on **What's new for
   KapiMart**: a list with one line per item, each with the assistant that
   noticed it, and a pane beside it for the item you select, with the
   sentence it was seen in and the file.
4. **Settle the disagreement first.** **Needs you** comes first, and the list
   opens with the disagreement selected. The pane shows its two sides next to
   each other. claude-code recorded a correction a person made to its draft,
   *log in* changed to *sign in*, seen in a sentence that says *sign in*.
   codex, later, recorded the opposite rule, and the sentence it quotes says
   *signing in* too. Read both, then click **Choose this side** on the one the
   evidence backs. That side is kept and the other dropped.
5. **Keep a name.** Under **Suggested**, select the suggestion that the store
   writes *KapiMart*, not *Kapi Mart*, with a click or with `j` and `k`, and
   press `a`. It moves to **Established**, marked "kept by you", and the
   selection moves on to the next suggestion.
6. **Change a word as you keep it.** Select the suggestion that the copy says
   *cart*, not *basket*, and press `c`. A field takes the place of the
   buttons, holding *cart*. Type the form you want the rule to ask for, such
   as *shopping cart*, and choose **Keep with this form**.
7. **Drop one.** Select a suggestion you would not make a rule, for example
   the observation about how the store copy addresses the shopper, and press
   `d`. It stops advising at once.
8. **Ask about the file the evidence came from.** Select any remaining
   suggestion and press `o`, or click the file under its quotation. **Ask**
   opens standing at that file, with the voice and the rules that apply there.
9. **See what the assistant reads.** Open **Assistant**. Under **The brief for
   a file**, pick one of the store's pages. The kept name and the word you
   changed are in the list of words to use, the same text `kapi context
   <path>` prints. Below it, **Rules files** shows the section kapi writes into
   `AGENTS.md` and `CLAUDE.md`, which the next session reads as it starts.
10. **Go back to before a session.** Open **History** and find the codex
    session. Click **Reset to before this session**. What codex recorded is
    set aside, and so is everything decided after it started, which includes
    your decisions in steps 4 to 7. What's new shows claude-code's suggestions
    as they first arrived.
11. **Take the reset back.** Nothing was erased: what the reset set aside stays
    in History, marked as reset, and the reset is recorded as yours. Resetting
    to before it brings everything back.

A check reports a suggestion and fails nothing. After step 5, `kapi check`
fails on any page that writes *Kapi Mart*, because the suggestion became a
rule.

## The same on the command line

```bash
kapi context review                         # what is waiting, one at a time
kapi context review --keep <id>             # keep a suggestion
kapi context review --keep <id> --use "shopping cart"   # keep it with another form
kapi context review --drop <id>             # drop one
kapi context review --choose <id>           # choose this side of a disagreement
kapi context reset --before <session>       # go back to before a session
```

[Growing context](/kapi/context-decisions) covers every decision and who may
make it.
