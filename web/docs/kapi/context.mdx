# Context

kapi stores writing guidance as **context**: approved terms, voice profiles and
previously approved wording. Writers and agents can consult it before drafting;
checks use the same context to evaluate the result.

Two questions describe the model:

- **Where does this content sit?** Every piece of content has a place in a
  context space: the _product_ it belongs to, the _channel_ it ships on, and any
  further axis the project declares for itself, such as a brand.
- **What governs that place?** A point in the space binds governance: a voice
  profile, a terms store, and the content memory of what has been said before.

This model supports consistent writing in a single language as well as
multilingual content. [Language overrides](#one-more-axis-language) refine
guidance within the same profile.

## The context space

Different parts of a project can need different guidance. A framework and its
companion app may have distinct voices; a marketing page and an API reference
may use different registers. Two structural axes identify where guidance applies:

| Axis        | Answers                        | In the recipe                                  |
| ----------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **product** | which thing this belongs to    | a key under `profiles:`                        |
| **channel** | which surface it ships on      | a `channel:` on the collection, as `product/channel` |

These two axes are **structural**: a key under `profiles:` _is_ a product, the
channels a profile lists _are_ the channels that product ships on, and a
collection names its point with one `channel:` reference. A project that sits
at a single point declares no profiles at all, and the whole of it is governed
by the project defaults.

```yaml title="kapi.yaml"
profiles:
  acme:
    channels: [docs, app, landing]
    voice:
      profile: acme

collections:
  - name: acme-docs
    channel: acme/docs
    base: web
    content:
      - path: "docs/**/*.md"
  - name: acme-landing
    channel: acme/landing
    content:
      - path: "site/pages/**/*.mdx"
```

This example defines one product with three channels. Add another key under
`profiles:` for a second product. Channel names are scoped to their product, so
two products can each have a `docs` channel with different guidance.

A collection's `channel:` is always written `product/channel`. A bare channel
name is a load error, even when exactly one product declares it; the error names
the qualified form to paste. A collection that binds no channel is governed by
the project's default point.

Products and channels are **slugs** (lowercase, stable, never translated), so
governance binds to an identifier that does not move when a term is reworded. A
profile or a channel may _carry_ a display concept, but resolution never looks
at it. The [project file reference](/reference/project-file#profiles-and-channels)
covers the field shapes in full.

### Declared axes

Beyond product and channel, a project can name the further dimensions its
content varies along. These axes are **declared**, under `defaults.coordinates`,
and every collection inherits them. A collection that sits elsewhere on one
axis overrides that axis alone with its own `coordinates:` and keeps the rest.
The most specific value wins, per axis.

```yaml title="kapi.yaml"
defaults:
  coordinates:
    brand: acme

collections:
  - name: acme-docs
    channel: acme/docs
    coordinates:
      mode: how-to
    content:
      - path: "docs/**/*.md"
```

Two axes have a conventional spelling. `brand` is the brand a point sits under,
coarser than product: a workspace has brands, a brand has products, a product
ships channels. `mode` is the kind of document at the point, in the sense the
Diátaxis scheme means: `tutorial`, `how-to`, `reference` or `explanation`.
The `mode` axis allows different guidance for tutorials and architecture notes. The coordinate map is open, and a project that
splits its content some other way declares the axis it needs.

Product and channel are never written under `coordinates:`. They are derived
from a collection's `channel:`, and a recipe that declares one there is
rejected at load, so the point a recipe computes and the point it states cannot
disagree.

## What governs a point

Three stores hold the context. They live in your
[workspace](/kapi/project-store), one set per project, and the recipe says which
of them applies where by naming it:

| Store              | Holds                                                | Bound by                          |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **voice profile**  | tone, register, preferred and forbidden vocabulary   | `defaults.voice` or a profile's `voice:`, by profile name |
| **terms**          | the approved word for each concept, per language     | the project's own terms, or a profile's `termstore:` |
| **content memory** | source/target pairs already produced and reviewed    | the project's own content memory  |

A profile binds a voice and a vocabulary to its whole product; the collection's
channel then refines the voice, selecting a channel-specific override inside the
bound profile when one exists. A point that declares no override of its own
falls back to the project default, the right answer for a voice that reads the
same everywhere.

Two recipe keys sit beside these bindings. A collection with no target language
can say so with `source_only: true`, so a run reads and checks it and writes
nothing back; the [project file reference](/reference/project-file#collections)
covers it. And the wording rules a voice profile carries under `vocabulary:`
have the same shape as the `term_rules:` a check or a translation step takes:
a term, its replacement and whether a violation fails the check. The [voice profile
reference](/reference/serialization/voice-profile#term-rules) lists the fields.

### Where a profile's context lives

All of it is in the project's stores, under one profile id per voice. A profile
that overrides the voice names the id it uses; one that overrides the vocabulary
names a terms store with `termstore:`. A profile that names neither is governed
by the project defaults.

```yaml title="kapi.yaml"
profiles:
  acme-labs:
    channels: [docs]
    voice:
      profile: acme-labs      # this product's voice
    termstore: labs-terms     # this product's vocabulary, a store by name
```

Content memory and the decision record belong to the project rather than to a
profile. Memory entries and approvals are associated with content units. See
[the project store](/kapi/project-store) for what each store holds.

[`kapi context snapshot`](/kapi/context-portability#writing-context-out) writes
all of it back out as files, one directory per profile under `profiles/` in the
directory you name,
and [`kapi context import`](/kapi/context-portability#reading-context-in) reads
that shape back in.

## How a point resolves

Governance resolves from the broadest binding toward the most specific, and the
most specific one that exists wins:

    { grade: "1", name: "project default", gloss: "defaults.voice · the project's own terms" },
    { grade: "2", name: "profile", gloss: "profiles.<name>.voice / termstore:" },
    { grade: "3", name: "channel", gloss: "the matching override inside the voice already selected" },
  ]}
  caption="A collection's point resolves up the ladder; an unbound collection stops at rung 1."
/>

The rungs behave differently:

- **Rung 2 selects a store.** The profile's `voice:` wins as written; failing
  that, the profile the store holds under the profile's own name; failing that,
  `defaults.voice`. Terms are one rung: the profile's `termstore:`, else the
  project's own terms.
- **Rung 3 refines what rung 2 selected.** A channel entry inside the bound
  voice profile replaces the tone, the style, or both, so a landing-page
  register is authored once beside the voice it varies rather than duplicated
  into a file per product-and-channel pair. A channel the profile has no entry
  for takes the base voice.

Vocabulary applies to every channel. The words a product will and will not use
are the same on every surface it ships on, which is why rung 3 moves the
register and leaves the term list alone.

kapi validates governance declarations when loading the recipe. It rejects
unknown channels, unqualified channel references, invalid slugs and voice
bindings that name two sources.

## Governance can have a lifetime

A profile can declare from when until when it is the one in force. The bounds
are a date or an RFC3339 instant, and the window is half-open: `valid_from` is
inclusive, `valid_to` exclusive, so a `valid_to` date excludes that whole day.

```yaml title="kapi.yaml"
profiles:
  acme-relaunch:
    channels: [landing]
    voice:
      profile: acme-relaunch
    valid_from: 2026-09-01
    valid_to: 2026-12-01
```

A profile outside its window **stops governing**. Resolution falls through to
the next binding (the collection's channel, then the project default) exactly
as if the expired one were not declared, so a seasonal voice applies only within its configured dates. The same instant governs a whole run, so a long convergence
cannot cross a boundary halfway through.

The switch is always reported. The first time a run, a check or a push resolves
through a lapsed profile it says so:

```
governance: profile "acme-relaunch" expired 2026-12-01; governing with the project default
```

`kapi context search` reads every bounded profile against the current time and
labels it `active`, `upcoming` or `expired`, so a writer can see that the
guidance they are about to follow has a start or an end date before it lapses.

The same half-open window scopes an individual term, which is what lets a
vocabulary survive a rename: _recognise the old name until March_ is a different
instruction from _never use it_, and a context answer distinguishes them.

## Two questions

Because the stores govern one point together, you do not ask them one at a time.
You ask by **location** or by **content**, and kapi reads whichever stores hold
the answer.

| Question | Ask | Over [MCP](/reference/mcp) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| what applies **here**? | `kapi context <path>` | read `context://<path>` |
| what do we know about **this**? | `kapi context search <query>` | call `context_search` |

### What applies here

Point `kapi context` at a file before you write it. The answer is a short brief:
the voice in force, one list of the words to use and to avoid there, and what has
been suggested but not yet established. It is written to be read by a person or
dropped into a model's context.

```bash
kapi context docs/billing.md
```

```text
# Writing docs/billing.md

Voice: Fernwell. Plain, second person, for studio owners who are not accountants. Short sentences.

Say this, not that:
- Fernwell Ledger, not "the Ledger": The product. Always the full name in prose.
- person, not "seat"

Suggested, not yet established:
- Quickcast is the forecast feature, always one word, capital Q (claude-code, seen in README.md)

Nothing else is recorded for this file. If you notice a name or spelling the project keeps to, record it with context_observe (or `kapi context observe`).
```

The word list merges the terms bound at the point with the voice profile's
vocabulary, so a rule held in both places is one line. The voice shows only the
fields the profile sets. `kapi voice guide <path>` prints the full profile.

Context queries, runs, checks and pushes use the same governance resolver. The
result includes content-item channel overrides and profile validity windows. The answer
is for the file's own content. An item that declares a file for its comments
alone governs only those comments, so the answer comes from the next item that
claims the file, or from the project's default point.

With no file in hand (an ad-hoc draft, a starter pack), name a profile instead:

```bash
kapi context --profile marketing
```

`--explain` adds how the answer was reached: the point the file sits at, the
recipe line that bound the voice, the project and workspace revision that
answered, and every note. `--json` gives all of it as data. Over MCP the two
addresses are resources rather than tools, because reading what already applies
at a place takes no arguments; `context://docs/billing.md` is the same text, and
`context://docs/billing.md?format=json` is the same document `--json` prints.

### What do we know about this

The other question takes a word or a phrase and asks it of every store the
project binds:

```bash
kapi context search "sign-in"
```

Results include preferred terms, discouraged terms and their replacements, and
previously approved wording. They are grouped by kind and identify any store
that could not be read.

Results also show term usage counts from the context graph built by the last
`kapi up`. A newly added term has no recorded uses until the next extraction.
The desktop explorer and concept page use the same counts.

Approved wording that contains a discouraged term is flagged. Use `--locale` to
restrict terms to one language and `--json` for structured output matching the
MCP tool response.

### An answer with nothing in it

A project with no recorded context returns an explicit empty result:

```text
# Writing docs/billing.md

Nothing is recorded for this file yet. Write as the surrounding files do.

While you read, record the names and spellings this project keeps to, such as a product or feature name, with context_observe (or `kapi context observe`).
```

The response invites an agent to record observations as suggestions
([Growing context](/kapi/context-decisions)). If the checkout contains
unimported context files, it instead starts with a notice asking a person to
run `kapi context import`.

The JSON carries the same fact as a `coverage` value, so a script or an
assistant can act on it. It counts what is in force at the point: the voice
profile, the terms bound there, and the rules you have confirmed
([Growing context](/kapi/context-decisions)). A search counts the terms it
matched and the prior wording it found.

| `coverage` | What stood behind the answer |
| --- | --- |
| `covered` | two or more of them |
| `thin` | one of them, or candidates alone |
| `empty` | nothing |

Unreviewed candidates raise coverage from `empty` to `thin`, but cannot produce
a `covered` result. The response reports the number awaiting review in
`kapi context log`.

`coverage` describes the answer rather than the project, so a search for a word
nobody here has written about is `empty` however much the project holds
elsewhere.

### Every answer says what it read

Each answer records the project it came from, the revision of your workspace it
was read at, and whether the content kapi holds still matches the files on disk.
`--explain` prints them:

```text
Read from project `prj_7k2n…` at workspace revision 41, content current.
```

In the JSON the same three sit under `provenance`. Two answers carrying one
revision were read from one state of your context, which is what lets you
compare them. A `stale` answer means files changed since kapi last read them, so
counts taken over your content describe the files as they were; `kapi up` reads
them again.

## Context throughout the writing process {#two-directions-around-the-writer}

Use context before, during and after writing:

- **Before**: the voice guide and the approved terms go into a writer's context
  (a person's, or an agent's), to guide the draft. The [agent skill](/kapi/get-started/use-with-claude)
  injects them automatically; `kapi context <path>` and `context_search` fetch
  them on demand.
- **During**: agents can record observations and corrections as suggestions
  for review. See [Growing context](context-decisions.mdx).
- **After**: `kapi check` scores the result block by block against the same
  stores, with an exit code, so the rules hold in the editing loop and again in
  CI with no assistant present.

A rule an agent proposes is a **candidate**. It is reported by checks, it fails
none of them, and it binds when you confirm it. Confirming writes it into the
project's store through the same route `kapi apply` takes, and records the
decision beside the evidence, so `kapi context log` is the review surface for
the _rules_. Where the decisions are already made, `kapi apply` lands them as
typed change-set entries directly.

## One more axis: language

Adding `target_languages` to the recipe adds no governance axis. A translated
block sits at the point its source sits at, and the same profile governs it:
the voice bound at `acme/landing` is the voice its Norwegian landing page is
written in, and the terms bound at `acme/docs` are the terms its German
documentation uses.

What a language adds is an override *inside* the voice profile, next to the
channel overrides: a `locales:` entry carries the formality, the point of view,
the vocabulary and the examples that hold for that language only. So the thing
you author for one language is the thing that governs every other, and the loop
that keeps the languages in step is [convergence](/kapi/convergence).
