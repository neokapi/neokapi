# Voice and corrections

A **voice profile** is a machine-readable description of how your content
should read: the vocabulary it should use, the terms it should avoid, and the
tone and style that make it recognizable. Most workspaces call their default
profile *brand*. A workspace with several audiences carries several profiles,
each bound at the point it governs. A profile works as a set of
[checks](https://neokapi.github.io/framework/checks). kapi and Bowrain run it
over content and report what drifts off-voice, in the way a test reports what
broke.

Bowrain aggregates repeated corrections into candidate rules. Reviewers can
inspect their evidence, preview their effect on stored content and promote them
into the workspace's terms. The **Voice** section of the
[Context hub](/server/context) provides this workflow.

## The loop

Repeated corrections can reveal gaps in a profile, such as an unwanted phrase
or a term reviewers consistently replace. Bowrain records these corrections for
review:

1. **Correct.** A person in the [review session](/server/review) or the editor,
   or an AI assistant working through [MCP](/cli/mcp), fixes content the
   profile should have caught. Bowrain records the correction with provenance:
   the original text, the fix, the voice dimension, the point it was made at,
   and who made it.
2. **Surface.** When the same `(original → corrected)` correction recurs across
   the project, it surfaces as a **candidate rule**. Candidates are recomputed
   live from the correction stream and reflect the current evidence.
3. **Preview.** Before a candidate is enforced, Bowrain computes its **blast
   radius**. It runs the rule over your stored content and shows how many
   blocks it would newly flag, how many it resolves, and how many of the new
   findings fail a check.
4. **Promote.** A reviewer **promotes** the candidate. Bowrain writes it into
   the workspace's terms as a term rule and records who promoted it and when.
   From then on, a rule-based check enforces it on every generation, in every
   language the terms cover. The promoted rule is **concept-backed**: it
   references the [concept](/server/context#concepts) its term denotes, so the
   rule and the term it governs share one identity in the Context hub.
   Promoting a voice rule is a governed edit, and a concept's
   [story](/server/context#the-concept-story) shows the rules that reference it.
5. **Monitor.** Bowrain tracks aggregate compliance and raises a **drift**
   alert when it declines.

## Rules inherit and expire

A rule is bound at a point and applies to everything beneath it until a
narrower profile overrides it. A rule can also carry a validity: it starts and
stops on a date. Use validity windows for campaign vocabulary or a rename's
transition period.

## Progressive autonomy

Every profile starts fully manual: each candidate waits for a person to promote
it. When a team trusts the candidates a dimension produces, it can raise that
dimension's **auto-promote threshold**. A correction that pushes a term past
the threshold then promotes its rule automatically and announces it. You turn
autonomy on per profile, one dimension at a time.

## Where it shows up

The browser, an AI assistant and the command line all work with the same voice
profiles:

- **Web**: each voice profile's review lists its candidates beside a detail
  pane, in groups: needs you, suggested, approved, established and rejected. A
  phrasing your team corrected to more than one form waits under needs you
  until you choose the form. Select a candidate to see the corrections behind
  it and who decided it, then promote, change or reject it from the pane's
  toolbar, or press `a`, `c` or `d`. Choose a project in the page header to
  preview a candidate's blast radius over that project's content. The Context
  hub's [dashboard](/server/context#the-dashboard-and-the-decision-inbox)
  links each profile's review and marks a drifting project on its row.
- **MCP**: an AI assistant can read the candidates its own corrections
  produced, preview a candidate's impact, and promote it.
- **CLI / CI**: a promoted rule is an ordinary
  [check](https://neokapi.github.io/framework/checks). `kapi` enforces it
  locally and in CI through its exit code, without contacting the server.

The open engine detects and scores. Bowrain keeps the decisions, profile
versions, provenance, autonomy settings and drift history, and provides the
screens that work with them.

For larger voice changes, such as banning a term across the workspace or
swapping a preferred term, the [Context](/server/context) hub groups the
voice-rule edits into a reviewable **change-set**. Bowrain measures its
[blast radius](/server/context#experiments-change-sets-and-pilots) before
anyone approves it.
