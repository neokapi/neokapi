# Terms

The terms explorer is the **Concepts** view of the [Context](/server/context)
hub. You use it to keep vocabulary consistent across locales. Each concept is
one meaning, with terms in several languages, a lifecycle status for each term,
and a domain.

The server keeps terms and content memory for every project in the workspace,
so the team has one approved record of its wording.

In the Context hub, each concept also carries typed **relations** to other
concepts (what replaced it, what to use instead, which competitor it stands
against), per-market validity, observations, comments and a
[story](/server/context#the-concept-story) of its changes. The sections below
cover everyday term management. [Context](/server/context) covers relations
and governance.

## Opening the Concepts page

Click **Context** in the main sidebar, then **Concepts**. The **Terminology**
button in a project's source view opens the same page, because every project
shares the workspace's terms. A link to one concept, from Activity, the
dashboard or a review, opens the list with that concept selected.

## The list

The page is a list of concepts beside a detail pane. Each row shows the term the
concept is headed by, that term's status, and how many languages the concept has
terms in. A concept is headed by its term in the language most of your projects
are written in.

- **Search**, at the top of the list, matches term text as you type.
- **Status** narrows the list to concepts with a term in that status.
- **Market** narrows it to one market. It appears once the workspace defines
  markets.
- **Show more**, at the foot of the list, reads the next page.

The server returns each search a page at a time, so a workspace with thousands
of concepts searches as quickly as one with ten. While the list has focus, the
arrow keys, `j` and `k` move the selection.

A change-set waiting for review sits above the list, with **Review** to open it.
Proposed concepts join the list once their change is approved.

## Term lifecycle statuses

Each term carries a lifecycle status that shows its approval level:

| Status         | Meaning                                            |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Preferred**  | The recommended term; use this one                 |
| **Approved**   | Accepted for use                                   |
| **Admitted**   | Acceptable but not preferred                       |
| **Deprecated** | Retired; replace it with the term to write instead |
| **Proposed**   | Under review, not yet approved                     |
| **Forbidden**  | Must not be used                                   |

## The detail pane

Select a concept to see it beside the list:

- its definition;
- **Write instead**: the deprecated and forbidden forms, each with the term to use
  in its place;
- **By language**: its terms in each language, the source language first, each
  with its status and its other forms, and the markets that cover the language;
- **Where it came from**: its domain, voice profile and coordinates, whether it
  belongs to one project or the whole workspace, and who added it and changed it
  last;
- **Relations** to other concepts; select one to open it;
- **History**, **Discussion** and **Evidence**: the concept's
  [story](/server/context#the-concept-story), the comments on it, and the
  observations recorded for it.

## Editing concepts

The actions for the selected concept are in the pane's toolbar:

- **Approve** sets the term the concept is headed by from proposed to approved.
  It appears while that term is proposed.
- **Edit** sets the status of each of the concept's terms.
- **Relate** adds a [relation](/server/context#relations) to another concept. The
  cross on a relation removes it.

How an edit applies depends on whether it is governed:

- **Ordinary edits**, such as adding or correcting a translation, adjusting a
  domain, or moving a term to a non-governed status, apply directly.
- **Governed edits**, such as setting or clearing a term's `forbidden` or
  `preferred` status, setting or clearing a concept's do-not-translate flag,
  recording a `replaced_by` relation, or deleting a concept, are bundled into a
  reviewable [change-set](/server/context#experiments-change-sets-and-pilots)
  whose blast radius is measured before anyone approves it. When the server
  refuses a governed edit, the page offers to open **Changes**, where the edit
  goes into a change-set.

A concept created with its do-not-translate flag set is governed too. Promoting
an entity marked do-not-translate, approving a context-scan term marked that
way, or adding such a term through the MCP `term_add` tool proposes the concept
in a change-set of its own, and the concept appears once that change-set
merges.

## Adding concepts

Concepts arrive from your projects and from review:

- `kapi push` sends a project's terms to the workspace. Ordinary terms apply
  directly; governed ones arrive as a change-set waiting for review.
- **Mark as term** in a review session, or approving a term a
  [context scan](/server/context-scan) proposed, creates the concept.

To load terms from a spreadsheet or a TBX file, import them into a project with
`kapi terms import`, then push the project.

## Deleting concepts

Deleting a concept is a governed action. The delete goes into a
[change-set](/server/context#experiments-change-sets-and-pilots), and the
concept is removed when that change-set is approved and merged. A concept that
other content references is therefore always reviewed before it goes.

## Terms in the editor

Terms are also used within the editor:

- **Context panel**: shows per-block term matches with source terms, target suggestions, status badges, and domain labels
- **Term checks**: validate that a target uses the terms in force at its point

When a block is selected in the editor, the context panel looks up matching
terms and displays them alongside memory matches. See
[The editor](/server/translation-editor) for details on the context panel.

## Concept model

The terms system is concept-oriented, in the manner of the TBX (TermBase
eXchange) standard:

- A **concept** represents a single meaning or notion
- Each concept has **terms** in one or more languages
- Terms have **metadata**: lifecycle status, domain, and notes
- Concepts are scoped to the workspace and shared across all projects

The framework owns this concept and term model; for its architecture, see the
[framework terms reference](https://neokapi.github.io/framework/terminology).

On the platform, the same concepts are the nodes of the
[context graph](/server/context), with typed relations, per-market validity,
observations, comments and governed change-sets. To model *how concepts
relate*, start at [Context](/server/context).
