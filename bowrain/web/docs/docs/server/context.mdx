# Context

Use the **Context** hub to manage writing guidance and terms for a workspace.
It shows where terms apply, their history and relations, and the effects of
proposed changes. Profiles can be scoped to a brand, product, channel or other
coordinate.

This page explains the hub and its approval process. See
[Voice and corrections](/server/context-voice) and [Terms](/server/terminology)
for detailed workflows. To derive initial proposals from existing content, run a
[context scan](/server/context-scan).

## Profiles

The hub opens on **Profiles**: one card per point the workspace's content sits
at. A point is a coordinate, a product, a channel, a brand, a market, and a
profile is what governs it. The workspace's own point, where content that
declares no coordinates sits, is labelled **Brand**.

Each card shows the applicable voice profile, vocabulary, stored check results,
pending reviews and custodians. Check results include scores and findings for
the blocks checked. A point with no results is marked as unchecked.
Opening a card shows these details and the collections and projects at that point.

Check standing is scoped by the **voice**, not by the point: a stored check
records the voice it resolved through, which is the narrowest coordinate the
check itself carries. Two points sharing one voice therefore share the number,
and the card says as much.

A profile is derived, never stored. A [`kapi push`](/cli/commands/push) declares
each collection's coordinates and the voice they resolved to; grouping the
collections by point recovers the profiles. A recipe's own `profiles:` regions
never cross the wire, since the project resolves them and sends the outcome, so
one broad profile governing two points reads here as the two points it governs.

### Who holds a point

Custodians are members with a `manage_voice` or `manage_terms` grant at the point
or an ancestor. The card lists their grants and scopes. Points without a
custodian are marked as unassigned; this does not prevent content processing.
Custodian assignments also determine custodian seat usage. See
[Members and roles](/server/members-and-roles#custodians-and-points).

### Axes

Projects declare axes in their recipes. Approving an axis proposed by a
[context scan](/server/context-scan) records a pending recipe change. The next
`kapi pull` writes a declared axis to `defaults.coordinates.<axis>` in
`kapi.yaml` for review and commit. A structural product or channel axis is
approved for a specific collection and written to its `channel:` field.

The server accepts content at the new coordinate after the updated recipe is
pushed. It rejects coordinates whose axes are absent from the recipe.

### Channel names

Projects can use different names for the same channel. Bowrain identifies
possible matches across a workspace and proposes them for review.

Accepting a proposal records that the names refer to the same channel.
Dismissing it records that they are distinct. Either decision prevents the pair
from being proposed again. These decisions leave recipe slugs and local
coordinate resolution unchanged.

## Concepts

The unit the hub curates is the **concept**, the language-neutral notion
Bowrain already uses for [terms](/server/terminology): each carries terms
across locales, every term with a lifecycle status, plus a domain and a
definition. A forbidden term is a concept whose term carries the `forbidden`
status rather than a separate kind of object. Voice vocabulary rules reference
the same concepts, so terms and voice cannot drift apart.

The **Concepts** section is a list of concepts beside a detail pane. Each row
shows the term a concept is headed by, that term's status, and how many
languages the concept has terms in. Search the list, or narrow it by term status
or market, and select a concept to see beside the list:

- its **terms** in each language, each with its lifecycle status, and the
  deprecated and forbidden forms with the term to write instead;
- **where it came from**: its domain, voice profile and coordinates, whether it
  belongs to one project or the whole workspace, and who added it and changed it
  last;
- its **relations**: the concepts one hop away;
- and its **history**, the **discussion** on it and the **evidence** recorded
  for it, one tab each.

Select a related concept to open it in the pane. A link to one concept, from
Activity, the dashboard or a review, opens the list with that concept selected.

## Relations

Concepts connect to one another through typed **relations**, drawn from the
framework's SKOS-aligned vocabulary. They fall into a few families: hierarchy (a
concept is broader than, narrower than, or part of another), succession (one
concept is replaced by another), guidance (use this one instead of that one),
cross-scheme equivalence, and stance (this concept is a competitor's). The
exact label set is defined by the framework's terms model; see the
[framework terms reference](https://neokapi.github.io/framework/terminology)
for the canonical list.

A concept's detail pane lists its **direct relations**, one line each: the kind
of relation, the concept at its other end, and an arrow for its direction.
Selecting the other concept opens it. **Relate**, in the pane's toolbar, adds a
relation to another concept, and the cross on a line removes one. An ordinary
relation applies directly; a succession (`replaced_by`) relation is a governed
edit that travels through a [change-set](#experiments-change-sets-and-pilots)
before it lands.

Every relation can carry a **validity**: a half-open time interval plus free tags
evaluated against a query-time scope. The same relation can hold in one market and
be absent in another, or be true only after a launch date, so the relations a
concept shows are the ones that apply *as of* the chosen date and *within* the
chosen market.

## Markets

A **market** is a workspace-defined scope, a name plus the locales it covers
(for example a `dach` market covering `de-DE`, `de-AT`, and `de-CH`). Markets give
the free validity tags a stable vocabulary: a term or relation scoped with
`market: dach` is interpreted, filtered, and rendered consistently everywhere. A
term's status can differ per market: a term can be forbidden in Germany and
permitted elsewhere. In a concept's detail pane, each language is labelled with
the markets that cover it, a relation scoped to a window shows the dates it holds
between, and the Concepts list narrows to one market. Plans also limit the number
of markets; see [Billing and credits](/server/billing-and-credits#markets-and-brands).

## The concept story

Every concept carries a **story**, its **History** tab in the detail pane: a
chronological record that merges the concept's revision history (each edit
produces an immutable snapshot with a human-readable summary, which is where
term-status transitions and relation changes are recorded), the **observations**
recorded against it, its **comment** threads, and the **change-sets** that touched
it, newest first, each with who acted and when. The story shows how a concept
changed and the evidence behind those changes, and the **Discussion** and
**Evidence** tabs beside it hold the comments and the observations in full.
Compliance scores are not part of the story; those are reported per project and
locale on the hub's [Dashboard](#the-dashboard-and-the-decision-inbox), not per
concept.

### Observations: what others say

An **observation** attaches external evidence to a concept: a competitor's
phrasing, customer language from a support ticket, a style-guide citation, a
regulatory requirement. Observations carry a kind, a quote, a source, and an
optional locale and market. They are evidence rather than rules: they inform
proposals and appear in the story, and they enforce nothing.

### Comments

Concepts carry threaded comments with @mentions, reusing the platform's
mention-notification machinery. Discussion lives where the decision will be
made, on the concept or on the change-set under review, and resolved threads
remain part of the story.

## Voice and corrections

The **Voice** section holds voice profiles and correction records. Repeated
corrections can produce candidate rules for a reviewer to promote into a profile.
Every promoted rule references the concept it governs. See
[Voice and corrections](/server/context-voice) for candidate review,
blast-radius previews and progressive autonomy.

## Experiments: change-sets and pilots

Some changes are too consequential to make in place. The hub's **Experiments**
section turns them into reviewable drafts.

A **change-set** is a named draft of edits to the graph and to the vocabulary:
create or update concepts, add or remove terms, change a term's status, add or
remove relations, add or remove concept-backed voice rules. Operations accumulate
in the draft; nothing touches the live graph until the change-set merges. Two
capabilities support evaluation before approval:

- **Blast-radius preview.** At any point the platform evaluates
  the draft against stored content across the workspace and reports which blocks,
  in which projects, collections, streams, and locales, would be newly flagged or
  resolved, with a source word count as a proxy for re-translation effort and a
  sample of affected blocks to inspect. The preview leaves stored content
  unchanged.
- **Pilots on selected streams.** Bind a change-set to one or more content
  [streams](/server/workspaces) to evaluate it before merging. Voice checks use
  the pilot's candidate profile on those streams. The trial report compares
  proposed term changes without applying them to ordinary term checks, which
  continue to use workspace terms. Merging applies the operations and
  removes the pilot overrides. Abandoning removes the overrides without applying
  the draft.

## Tiered governance

Edits are classified as **ordinary** or **governed**. Only governed edits require
approval by another person.

**Ordinary edits**, such as definitions, notes, observations, comments, proposed
terms and non-status term metadata, apply directly, produce a revision, and land
in the audit trail.

**Governed edits**, such as setting a term's status to `forbidden` or
`preferred`, removing such a status, succession (`replaced_by`) relations,
concept-backed voice rule changes, and merging any change-set, travel through a
change-set and require at least one approval from **someone other than the
author** (separation of duties). Term-status transitions are additionally
checked against the framework's status-transition policy, so a forbidden term
cannot quietly return to preferred without passing through a governed transition.

## The dashboard and the decision inbox

The **Dashboard** opens on the workspace's counts: projects, concepts,
suggested rules, what needs you, active experiments and voice profiles.
**What's new** sits below them and lists what waits for a decision first. A
change-set in review or approved shows who proposed it, how long it has waited
and the blast radius stored when it was submitted. Its button opens the
change-set, where a reviewer approves or rejects it. A voice profile whose
team's corrections suggest rules has a row that counts them by kind, and its
button opens the profile's [review](/server/context-voice#where-it-shows-up).
The change-sets that moved most recently follow.

Each project then has one row: its voice profile, its latest compliance score
against that profile's bar, the trend, whether it is drifting, and when it was
last checked. One project's compliance over time, terms by status, each
language's coverage, the markets and the most recently changed concepts sit
below the projects.

The **Activity** section is the workspace's record of work on its context, as a
list of sessions beside a detail pane. A session is one person's run of work:
their operations, close together in time. Each row names who worked, the time
and the number of operations, under the day it happened. The detail pane lists
the session's operations, each with an icon for its kind, and its facts: when it
ran, what it recorded, and the reviews it decided, so who approved, rejected or
merged a change stays on the record. Selecting an operation about a concept or a
change-set opens it, and the toggles in the header narrow the record to changes,
concepts, evidence or comments.

The record covers concepts created and updated, statuses changed, relations
gained and lost, observations and comments added, change-sets submitted,
approved, merged, and abandoned, and pilots started and stopped. It is the
workspace-wide counterpart to a single concept's story.

## One hub, many surfaces

The Context hub is the same in the web app and the desktop app. The desktop app
remains a working copy of the server: it reads the same API, and concepts and
relations are edited inline in the hub against the server, never authored
offline.

Terms also reach a project's local files and CI through ordinary sync. A
[`kapi pull`](/cli/commands/pull) snapshots the workspace's governed concepts and
their relations into the project's local terms store, and
[`kapi check --ship`](/cli/use-cases/brand-terminology-ci) then gates offline
against exactly the terms the hub shows, the pattern documented in
[Gate governed terms in CI](/cli/use-cases/brand-terminology-ci). A
[`kapi push`](/cli/commands/push) sends local term edits back: ordinary
edits apply directly, while governed edits become a submitted change-set
proposal, so the same separation of duties holds whether an edit is made in the
hub or from a project.

AI assistants retrieve the same approved context through the bowrain plugin's
[MCP](/cli/mcp) tools, concept search, a concept's story, and change-set status,
so an assistant consults the hub's terms rather than guessing.
